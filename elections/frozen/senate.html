Method, parameters and sources
  • Polling average. Every poll since Aug 1 is counted once (aggregator averages are never used as inputs). Weight = recency (half-life 14 days) × √(sample size, capped at 1500) × population (LV 1.0, RV 0.8, adults 0.6) × 0.5 for campaign/party-sponsored polls × 0.85 if the numbers were only seen in a secondary table. A pollster's older releases count at 0.35× per newer release, so frequent pollsters don't dominate. Sponsored polls are shifted 3.5 points against their sponsor and one-party pollsters 1.5 points. Sponsored Senate polls ran 3.6 pts toward sponsor vs nonpartisan polls in same race (538 recompute); Silver uses ~4.
  • Fundamentals prior. 2024 presidential margin + national shift of +10.2 (Generic ballot D+8.7 (Oct 2026 avg) minus 2024 national presidential margin R+1.5) + 2 points for an incumbent. It is blended with the polls by precision, so it matters only where polling is thin. Prior SD 12. Not used for independent challengers.
  • No partisan bias assumed. Zero in baseline: direction does not persist; midterm averages near zero (2018 +0.3, 2022 +1.2 nonpartisan / -0.9 all). Tested as scenarios instead. A state's past polling miss predicts its next one only weakly (correlation about 0.2), so no state-specific adjustments are applied.
  • Correlated error. Each simulation draws one national error (SD 3; SD of cycle-level Senate poll bias 1998-2022 = 2.7 (538 raw-polls.csv recompute); bias correlation between cycles 0.17), one per region (SD 1.75), and one per race (SD 3.5 plus sampling noise of 4/√(effective polls), plus 0.15 × undecided %, plus 2.5 for independent challengers). All errors are Student-t with 6 degrees of freedom, so big misses are more likely than under a normal curve.
  • Ranked choice. Maine and Alaska use RCV. Where a pollster published final-round numbers they are used; otherwise minor candidates' first choices are transferred (Alaska: Heikes 80% to Sullivan, Sullivan Jr. split evenly).
  • Tiers come from win probability: Safe ≥ 99%, Likely ≥ 90%, Lean ≥ 75%, Tilt ≥ 60%, else Toss-up. The 6-point rule (10 in the original sheet) is kept only to flag which races get a deep dive.
  • Not modeled: turnout scenarios beyond what the polls capture; late candidate events; Louisiana and Georgia runoffs (both are two-candidate races, so they cannot trigger).
